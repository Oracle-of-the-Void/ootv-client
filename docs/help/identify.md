# Identifying L5R cards

Which arc and which set an old Legend of the Five Rings card comes from, read off the card itself:
the **bug** tells you the arc, and the **border** (before Gold Edition) or the **set code** (from Gold
Edition on) tells you the set.

## The bugs: which arc {#bugs}

From Jade Edition (1998) on, each card prints a small symbol, the "bug", in a lower corner of its text
box: the kanji of the arc it was printed for. A card with a bug was made to be played in that arc.

## Two bugs {#two-bugs}

Sets released near the end of an arc often print two bugs, one in each corner: the arc that's
ending and the one about to start (Gold and Diamond, Ivory and Twenty Festivals, Onyx and Shattered
Empire, ...). The card was made for both.

## No bug {#no-bug}

The first arc, Clan Wars (the Imperial arc, 1995–1998), had no bugs at all: every card from
Pre-Imperial Edition to Scorpion Clan Coup has none. Some later products also print none: tokens,
some promotional cards and reprint collections. For those, the border or the set code says where the
card is from.

Faint or hidden bugs exist: a few starter-deck reprints print the bug very lightly, and on some cards
the text runs over it. Look closely before deciding a card has none.

## Bugs and legality {#legality}

The bug shows what a card was printed for. Whether it's legal to play is a separate question: cards
were banned from arcs after they were printed, and some formats allow cards from other arcs. The card
page's legality shows the current answer, each arc with its bug beside it (redrawn as a wax seal, the
same style for every arc, so not exactly as printed). A Detailed Search result shows the bugs of the arcs
the card is legal in under its versions.

## Which set: from Gold Edition on {#set-code}

From Gold Edition (2001) on, every card prints its set's code, its number in the set and its rarity
along the bottom edge, so the set can be read straight off the card. Rarity symbols: circle common,
diamond uncommon, star rare, triangle promo, hollow diamond premium (from Emperor Edition), star in a
circle fixed.

## Which set: the Imperial arc borders {#imperial-borders}

Before Gold Edition nothing on the card names the set: the outer border does. Look at the black band
between the card's edge and the frame, all the way round. These strips are the bottom edge of each
set's border.

## Look-alikes {#look-alikes}

- **Hidden Emperor 1–6 and The Dark Journey Home** share one border: the border can't tell them apart.
- **Scorpion Clan Coup** scrolls 1, 2 and 3 are identical.
- **Plain black borders**: Battle of Beiden Pass, Top Deck Learn to Play, and the dark ones (Ambition's
  Debt, Pearl Edition, Siege of Sleeping Mountain) are easiest to confuse. Look at the border in good
  light, and at the back: Battle of Beiden Pass has the old card back.
- **Honor Bound, Fire and Shadow and Soul of the Empire** use the same net of cells in different
  colours: dark red, olive and bright green.
